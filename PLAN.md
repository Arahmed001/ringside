# Ringside: architecture and roadmap

A boxing database with better design and far more analytics than BoxRec. This file covers what exists, how the pieces fit, and what comes next.

## 1. Principles
1. **Own the database.** Vendors feed our tables through an adapter and nothing renders from a live vendor call. A vendor can then change pricing or disappear without breaking the site, as long as the licence allows storing the data (see §4).
2. **Provenance on everything.** Every photo shows its licence and author. Every rating is labelled as ours, unofficial.
3. **No scraping, no guessing.** BoxRec forbids scraping. Images come from Wikimedia (free licences) or from the vendor feed. The headshot matcher only accepts a match it can verify, and otherwise shows nothing.
4. **Everything degrades gracefully.** No AI key: search and scouting reports fall back to rules. No photo: a generated portrait. No poster: generated art.

## 2. Current architecture
```
provider adapter ──► ingest ──► SQLite (node:sqlite) ──► world cache ──► server components
 demo | licensed      │             boxers/events/bouts       │            (pages, SSR)
                      │             rating_history            ├─► lib/rankings   (division top 15, 90-day movement)
                      ├─► Elo recompute                       ├─► lib/analytics  (heatmaps, upsets, streaks)
                      └─► media resolver (optional)           ├─► lib/model      (logistic win-probability, pure)
                          Wikidata → Commons                  ├─► lib/style      (archetypes, similarity, PCA map)
                                                              └─► lib/ai         (NL search, scouting reports)
```
- **Stack:** Next 16 (App Router, async `params`/`searchParams`), TypeScript, Tailwind 4, `node:sqlite`.
- **Divisions:** `lib/divisions.ts` holds the 17 official divisions with weight limits. `normalizeDivision()` maps vendor spellings such as "Jr. Welterweight" to canonical names and throws on unknown input, so bad data fails at ingest.
- **Ratings:** Elo (K=24, +15% for stoppages) replayed chronologically on every ingest. Rankings are active fighters with 5+ bouts and a fight in the last 24 months.
- **Prediction:** a logistic regression over fighter differences (`lib/model.ts`). The same pure function runs on the server for default odds and in the browser for the sliders. Weights are hand-set until real results exist to fit them.
- **Images:** a real `photoUrl`/`posterUrl` from a feed or Wikimedia wins. Otherwise generated SVG portraits and posters (deterministic per fighter).

## 3. Media resolver (Wikimedia)
`lib/media/wikimedia.ts`, `lib/media/resolve.ts`, `scripts/resolve-media.ts`

A candidate is accepted only if **all** of these hold:
1. Wikidata lists it with occupation *boxer* (Q11338576).
2. Its birth year equals ours (exactly one boxer must match, so shared names are rejected as ambiguous).
3. It has an image (P18) on Commons that is not non-free, is a JPEG/PNG/WebP at least 250px wide, and carries an accepted licence (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA).

It stores author, licence and page URL in `boxer_media` and renders them under the photo. It is polite to the API: it sends a User-Agent with contact info (`WIKIMEDIA_CONTACT`), spaces requests 250ms apart, and backs off on 429/5xx. Highest-rated fighters are resolved first, and misses are retried after 45 days.
- **Cropping:** CSS `object-position: top` in a 4:5 frame. Face detection is a possible later step.
- **Hotlinking:** thumbnails are loaded from Commons. At scale, mirror them to our own storage (licences allow it).
- **Posters:** Wikimedia rarely has fight posters, so promo art must come from the vendor or promoters. Generated posters fill the gap.
- **Not used:** Google Custom Search for images. It returns other people's copyrighted work.

## 5. Interactive predictor
`lib/model.ts` and `components/MatchupLab.tsx`. Six terms (rating, reach, age past 34, ring rust past 12 months, power, chin). The page has presets (Balanced, Pure Elo, Old school, Father Time), a 0–3× weight slider per term, and what-if sliders for each fighter's rating, age, reach and layoff. ## 7. Status and known issues (2026-10-03)

**Verified (round 1):** type check and lint clean; production build passes; all 12 routes return 200 (404 for unknown fighter/division); no horizontal overflow at 390px on 10 pages; Next upgraded 16.2.6 to 16.3.8 (`npm audit`: 0 vulnerabilities, it was 1 critical and 2 high); interactive predictor checked in the browser (age slider, presets and reset move the odds correctly; "Pure Elo" equals the Elo expectation); Wikimedia resolver checked against mocked Wikidata/Commons responses (14 decision cases + 17 licence strings) and its SQLite path (upsert, retry window, re-ingest keeps photos).

**Not verified:** the Wikimedia resolver against the real Wikimedia APIs (needs a `WIKIMEDIA_CONTACT`); text contrast on generated posters; behaviour in Safari or Firefox; any real vendor data.

**Known issues and dev notes**
- **Do not set `turbopack.root` in `next.config.ts`.** With it set, `next dev` hung at "Compiling /", repeatedly respawned `next-server` and leaked hundreds of `postcss.js` worker processes until the machine hit its process limit ("fork failed: resource temporarily unavailable"). It happened twice, and each time went away when the setting was removed. The cause inside Next is not isolated. If you see that error, run `pkill -f "ringside/node_modules/.bin/next"; pkill -f "ringside/.next/dev/build/postcss.js"`.
- Editing `lib/db.ts` while `next dev` is running can break the `node:sqlite` import until the server restarts.
- `node:sqlite` is experimental in Node 22 and prints a warning. Requires Node 22.5+.
- Demo data is generated on first run into `data/ringside.db`. After changing `lib/providers/demo.ts`, stop the server, delete `data/` and restart.
- The in-memory world is rebuilt when the calendar day changes or the database changes (another process committed, or a new ingest run was recorded), not on a timer; see §10. Anything that writes to the database from inside the server process without recording an ingest run should call `invalidateWorld()`.
- The demo league has fewer qualified contenders in some divisions (5 to 15 per division). Ranking rules require a winning record, so thin divisions list fewer than 15.
- Rankings, ratings and predictions are unofficial and computed from the (fictional) demo results.

## 8. Data landscape and data model (round 2, 2026-10-03)

### 8.1 What data actually exists
Measured, not assumed. Open sources cover fighter biography well and everything else poorly.

| Fact | Best open source | Coverage | Real route for the rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birth date / place, nationality, height, weight, image, cross-IDs | **Wikidata (CC0)** | ~19.6k boxers; birth date 17.4k, birthplace 12.2k, citizenship 16.4k, BoxRec ID 10.1k, image 3.5k, height 3.5k, weight 1.5k | n/a, already good |
| Fighter photos | **Wikimedia Commons** (CC BY / BY-SA / CC0 / PD) | follows Wikidata images | vendor-supplied |
| Bout records, results, belts, rankings | none | none | licensed feed (Boxing Data API, Box.Live, Sportbex) |
| **Trainers / managers / gyms, with dates** | none | Wikidata lists 654 boxing trainers but only ~16 have any student linked, and ~25 boxers any "student of" | BoxRec licensing enquiry; vendor fields (undocumented); editors; Wikipedia prose |
| **Weigh-in and fight-night weights** | US athletic commission result PDFs (Nevada, California) are public records | US only, PDF, no bulk API | commission PDFs downloaded by hand + a parser (not built); vendor fields (undocumented) |
| Judges, referees, scorecards | commission results; vendor | partial | same |
| Punch statistics | CompuBox (paid) | partial | licence |

Not usable: BoxRec (scraping prohibited; data licensed to partners only) and Kaggle "boxing" sets (no provenance or licence statement found; several are scrapes of the above). Nevada's site rejected automated requests, so commission files need to be downloaded manually.

**Wikidata gotcha (handled):** raw quantity values mix units. One property returned `1.74` (m), `70.5` (inches) and `173` (cm) for different boxers. The importer reads the unit-normalised fields (`psn:`) and drops implausible values.

### 8.2 Data model (SQLite; every optional table is filled only if the provider supplies it)
```
boxers      + birth_date, birth_place, residence, wikidata_id, boxrec_id, aliases, debut_date, retired_date
events      + promoter_org_id, broadcaster, attendance
bouts       + round_time, kd_red, kd_blue, odds_red, odds_blue, contract_lb, title_org_id, title_vacant
people      trainers, managers, judges, referees (one table; roles come from relationships)
orgs        gyms, promotions, sanctioning bodies, broadcasters
team_stints boxer ↔ person/org, role (head_trainer, assistant_trainer, strength_coach, cutman, manager, promoter, gym),
            start_date, end_date (NULL = current), source
weigh_ins   (bout, boxer): official_lb, fight_night_lb, limit_lb, made_weight, source
officials   bout ↔ referee/judge (+ seat);  scorecards: bout, judge, seat, red_score, blue_score
corners     who was actually in the corner for a bout
punch_stats (bout, boxer, round 0 = total): thrown/landed, power, jabs
wikidata_boxers  staging copy of the Wikidata import + match method
```
Provider contract: `lib/providers/index.ts`. Re-ingest is idempotent: stints and weigh-ins are replaced per `source`, so enrichment rows from other sources (e.g. `wikidata`) survive. Slugs are unique against rows that already exist.

### 8.3 Pipelines
- `npm run wikidata:import -- --limit 500` then `-- --enrich`: bulk biography import (batched SPARQL, one request per ~1.2s, needs `WIKIMEDIA_CONTACT`; resumable, see the note below); enrichment links our fighters by BoxRec ID, otherwise by normalised name + birth year when exactly one candidate exists, and fills **only** blank fields so a licensed feed always wins.
- The same import now also stages **International Boxing Hall of Fame IDs (P4474), Olympedia IDs (P8286) and awards (P166 with year)** in a second, separate query per batch (`--limit N` as before); `--enrich` copies the IDs onto linked fighters (blanks only) and rewrites each linked fighter's `honours` rows with `source = 'wikidata'` (rows from other sources are never touched).
- `npm run media:resolve`: photos; fighters already linked to Wikidata skip the name search.
- `npm run model:fit`: fits win-probability weights from the bouts in the database (§8.5).

### 8.4 New pages
`/bouts/[id]` (weigh-in, corners, officials, scorecards, knockdowns, odds, punch stats) · `/people` and `/people/[slug]` (trainer leaderboard and stables with timelines, managers, judges, referees) · `/orgs` and `/orgs/[slug]` (gyms, promotions, sanctioning bodies with current titleholders) · `/weights` · `/data` (field coverage, source registry, model report). Fighter profiles gain bio facts, a corner-and-camp timeline and a weigh-in chart. Search understands "trained by", "managed by", "promoted by", "out of the X gym", "born in", "missed weight", "new trainer".

### 8.5 Model fitting: what we learned
Features known before the fight (Elo gap, reach, age, layoff, KO rate, KO losses, experience, usual rehydration, fight-night weight edge, new-trainer flag, trainer's prior win rate), L2 logistic regression, time-ordered split (train on the first 75% of bouts, test on the last 25%).
On the demo league (4,760 training + 1,587 held-out bouts): refitting only the Elo scale cuts held-out log-loss from 0.6165 to 0.5813; adding every other feature reaches 0.5796, a gain of 0.0017 that is within noise, so the **parsimony rule** (a more complex model must beat a simpler one by at least 0.002) keeps "Elo refit". The planted weigh-in and trainer effects are too small to detect at this size (z of about 0.0 and 0.2). The app therefore applies only the fitted Elo scale (about 2.7× the plain expectation: ratings are compressed relative to true skill gaps) and leaves other terms hand-set. **Re-run `npm run model:fit` on real data**: it will say what is really predictive. (An earlier, smaller demo league recommended Elo refit for a different reason: extra features were slightly *worse* there. The recommendation can change as data grows; that is the point of re-running it.)

### 8.6 What the demo league plants (so analytics have something to find)
Trainer skill boosts (sd 45 Elo) · a fight-night weight edge (2.2 Elo per lb) · judges with home-fighter bias · referees with early-stoppage tendencies. These are simulator properties, not claims about boxing.

2. Ask BoxRec about data licensing (the one source that has trainers and weigh-ins at scale).

## 9. Real-data readiness and quality gates (round 3, 2026-10-03)

### 9.1 Real-world cases the model now handles
Found by reviewing the code against what real feeds contain; each would otherwise have failed an import or corrupted records.
- **Result methods:** KO, TKO, RTD (corner retirement), DQ, UD/MD/SD, TD (technical decision), DRAW, TDRAW (technical draw), NC. `lib/methods.ts` defines what each means for records: a corner retirement counts as a knockout (as on BoxRec), a DQ is a win but not a knockout, a no-contest is excluded from both records, technical results are scored over the rounds fought. `normalizeMethod` maps vendor spellings ("Decision - Split", "Technical Knockout", "N/C").
- **Women's boxing:** `sex` on every fighter; rankings, pound-for-pound, search and labels are separate ("Women's Welterweight"). Women never fight men in the demo.
- **Stance:** Orthodox, Southpaw, Switch.
- **Cancelled and postponed:** events and bouts carry a status. A cancelled bout on a live card is shown but never upcoming and never headlines; a postponed card stays on the calendar; cancelled cards never appear as results or calendar entries.
- **Pinned clock:** everything date-dependent goes through `lib/clock.ts`, so `RINGSIDE_NOW=2026-10-03` makes runs reproducible.

### 9.2 Data-quality gate
`sanitizeFeed` (lib/validate.ts) checks a feed **before** anything is written:
- **Errors drop the row** (and anything that depends on it): duplicate or missing IDs, unknown division or method, bad dates, a winner who was not in the bout, a result with no winner (or a draw with one), end round past the scheduled distance, references to fighters/events/bouts/people that do not exist, team stints with no person/organisation or with end before start.
- **Warnings keep the row and flag it:** scorecards that contradict the recorded result, incomplete or out-of-range scorecards (technical decisions are range-checked over the rounds fought), a made-weight flag that contradicts the scale, rehydration or weights that are implausible, a limit that doesn't fit the division, a fighter on two cards the same night, overlapping head trainers, implausible height/reach/debut age, punch stats that don't add up.
- **Info:** people or organisations linked to nothing.

Every ingest records its run and issues (`ingest_runs`, `ingest_issues`), shown on the Data page. Strict mode (abort before touching the database if any row has an error) is `ingest(db, provider, { strict: true })`.

**Evaluating a vendor sample:** convert it to the `FeedData` JSON shape (`lib/feed.ts`; any missing array is treated as empty), then `npm run data:check -- --file sample.json`. It prints rows in/kept, issues grouped by code with examples, and exits 1 if any row would be dropped. `BOXING_PROVIDER=file` with `BOXING_FILE=sample.json` loads such a file into the app.

### 9.3 Tests and CI
- `npm test` (Node's built-in runner, 96 tests, about two seconds): divisions, methods, model, validator rules, ingest (quarantine, strict mode, idempotency, slugs), world invariants (every record equals the results that count; league wins equal losses; rankings are sex-separate, active, winning-record), a hand-built calendar with every status combination, search parsing, the Wikimedia and Wikidata importers (mocked network, fixtures from real responses), the resolver's database path, the model fitter (recovers known coefficients), the parsimony rule, and the `data:check` CLI including its exit codes.
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs on every push and PR: `npm ci`, type check, lint, tests, `data:check` on the demo feed, production build; plus a non-blocking `npm audit`. The exact steps were replayed in a clean copy with a fresh `npm ci` and all passed. It has not yet run on GitHub itself.
- Test hygiene: a deliberate-breakage check confirmed the suite fails when a corner retirement stops counting as a knockout, when the validator keeps unknown divisions, when the age penalty has the wrong sign, when women fight men, and when cancelled bouts count as upcoming (that last one initially slipped through, which is why `tests/calendar.test.ts` exists).

### 9.4 Still open
Everything in §8.7, plus: the CI workflow's first real run on GitHub; a UI for the quality report beyond the Data page; amateur and multi-day (tournament) results; per-round scorecards; weigh-in video/photo evidence (not planned).

## 10. Scale test (round 4, 2026-10-03)

The demo league is 968 fighters and 7,755 bouts. A licensed feed could be 20 to 1000 times that, so the app was tested at 5× and 20× (19,360 fighters, 159,296 bouts, 1.1 million punch rows, 324 MB database) with `npm run bench -- --scale N [--keep]` (generation, validation, ingest, world build, the server-side work behind every page, and what each page ships to the browser) plus per-route HTTP timings against `next start`. Numbers are from one Apple-silicon Mac, Node 22, single process.

### 10.1 What it found, and what changed

| At 20× | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `/compare` HTML | 8.9 MB (every fighter in a `<select>`), 320 ms | 23 KB, 4 ms (type-ahead over `/api/fighters`) |
| `/map` HTML | 5.4 MB, 181 ms | 555 KB (82 KB gzipped), 50 ms (400 highest-rated per style, tuples, 3-decimal coordinates) |
| `/` HTML | 1.8 MB, 151 ms | 514 KB (44 KB gzipped), 81 ms (watchlist fetched for the few starred fighters; 12 posters in the strip) |
| `/events` HTML | 1.3 MB | 1.2 MB: 24 upcoming cards unless `?upcoming=all`; the rest is the 48 generated posters (see 10.3) |
| `/people?role=judge` | 735 ms per view | 5 ms warm (154 ms once per world) |
| `/analytics` | 457 ms | 10 ms warm (240 ms once) |
| `/orgs` | 351 ms | 5 ms warm (276 ms once; stats for the top 36 gyms and promotions only) |
| `/weights` | 280 ms | 8 ms warm (138 ms once) |
| `/data` | 278 ms | 5 ms (cached per database version) |
| `/people` (trainers) | 160 ms | 6 ms warm (231 ms once) |
| 4 simultaneous world builds | 4 separate builds, 16.2 s | 1 build, 3.9 s |

Every route is now under 100 ms warm at 20×, and at the demo size every route is under 30 ms (the same as before). Over HTTP, the first request after a restart still pays for the world build (8 concurrent cold requests all finished after 4.6 s; the benchmark puts the world build itself at 3.5 to 3.7 s).

What was done:
- **World cache** (`lib/world.ts`, `lib/db.ts`): the world is now valid until the calendar day changes or the database does (`PRAGMA data_version` moves when another process commits; the latest ingest run id covers a re-ingest here). The 10-minute timer is gone, so the 3.6 s blocking rebuild no longer happens every ten minutes. Callers that arrive together share one build (it used to be one build each). `eventById`, `boutById` and `officialsByPerson` replace per-request linear scans (judge stats, bout and event pages, official profiles, head-to-head).
- **Memoised aggregates** (`lib/memo.ts`): analytics, judge/referee/dispute tables, trainer leaderboard, weigh-in aggregates, the organisations index and the coverage queries are computed once per world (or database version), not per page view. The world is never mutated, so a rebuilt world starts with empty caches. Rule: results are shared, copy before sorting (the trainer table does).
- **Algorithms:** `upcomingEvents`/`recentEvents` walk back from the end of the date-ordered list instead of filtering every event; `finishHeat`, `byWeightClass` and `divisionWeights` are single passes (they re-scanned all bouts or weigh-ins once per division); the organisations index ranks on a cheap count and computes full stats for 36 cards.
- **Payloads:** fighter pickers search on the server (`lib/fighter-search.ts`, about 1 ms at 20,000 fighters, accent-insensitive, prefix matches first); a typed name still works without JavaScript because the server resolves it. The home watchlist asks `/api/watch` for just the starred slugs (capped at 100).
- **Generator** (`lib/providers/demo.ts`, `scale` option): quadratic loops removed and a gym-name infinite loop fixed (320 gyms from 96 plain name combinations spun forever at 5×). The scale-1 output is byte-identical (fingerprint SHA1 `a756f32cb042b1b216235aee10c1806e642b9dcf`, 968 fighters, 7,755 bouts).
- **Tests** (`tests/scale.test.ts`, `tests/calendar.test.ts`, 96 in all): shared build, rebuild on day change and on another connection's commit, no rebuild on a timer, every lookup map and memoised aggregate against the plain scan it replaced, type-ahead ranking and the no-JavaScript fallback, API bounds, style-map sampling, org index and weigh-in aggregates against the old formulas, a scaled feed that validates with unique names. Breaking each fix on purpose makes a test fail (checked for the cache key, the shared build, the calendar filters, the sampling order, the search ranking, gym names and the weigh-in pass).

### 10.2 Where the architecture runs out

| | 1× (demo) | 5× | 20× | extrapolated 100× |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bouts | 7,755 | 39,779 | 159,296 | ~800,000 |
| world build (blocking) | 0.12 s | 0.67 s | 3.7 s | ~19 s |
| world heap | 22 MB | 114 MB | 456 MB | ~2.3 GB |
| process RSS (bench process) | 179 MB | 589 MB | 1.7 GB | ~8 GB |
| database file | 14 MB | 79 MB | 324 MB | ~1.6 GB |
| ingest (validate, write, Elo) | 0.3 s | 2.1 s | 9.7 s | ~50 s |

Build time and memory are linear in bouts (the extrapolation is a straight line, not a measurement). The single-process, in-memory world is **comfortable to about 50,000 bouts, tolerable to about 200,000** (a 4 s pause at midnight and after each ingest, 2 GB of RAM), and **not viable beyond roughly 500,000** (a 10 s freeze of the whole server on every rebuild, which no cache tuning fixes because the build is synchronous). Decision rule for the vendor choice: if the licensed feed has fewer than about 150,000 bouts (an active-fighter or last-decade subset does), stay on this design and SQLite; if it carries full history for 400,000+ fighters, the world must shrink to a hot set (active fighters, their bouts, current rankings) with the rest read from the database per page, and the database should move to Postgres at the same time (concurrent writers for ingest, real indexes for search, no 2 GB process). That is a substantial rewrite of `lib/world.ts` and of every page that scans `w.bouts`; it is not started because the vendor and its volume are not known.

### 10.3 Still open from this round
- **Cold start and first request after a rebuild** block for the build time above. **Fixed in §15** (warm-up at start and just after midnight UTC); the build itself is still synchronous, so a 160k-bout world still freezes the server for about 4 s while it runs.
- **Generated art is heavy:** each generated portrait is about 9 KB of inline SVG and each poster about 25 KB, so `/rankings` is 892 KB and `/events` 720 KB to 1.2 MB of HTML at every scale (50 to 80 KB gzipped, which the server does send). **Fixed in §15.**
- The per-request linear scans that remain are small (judge profiles use `officialsByPerson`, but `/orgs/[sanctioning body]` still filters all bouts for its title fights; not measured, a single pass), `/boxers` filters and sorts everyone per search (8 ms), and the trainer table and organisation index take 230 to 280 ms once per world.
- Fighter search is a linear scan; past about 200,000 fighters it needs an index (or Postgres trigram search).

## 11. Step 1 of the feature list: Arabic/English site, SEO foundation, ⌘K (round 5, 2026-10-03)

**Bilingual, whole site.** English stays at the bare path, Arabic is `/ar/...` (`proxy.ts` rewrites English requests into `app/[locale]/` and 308-redirects `/en/...` so each page has one URL per language; crawlers are never redirected by `Accept-Language`). Every page, component, API answer, search result, share image and AI scouting prompt is localised; details and the writing rules are in `docs/i18n.md`.
- **Text:** the English sentence is the key (`t("Find a fighter")`), Arabic lives in `i18n/ar.json` (783 keys, 0 missing), a missing entry falls back to English. Plurals use Arabic's six forms; gendered sentences have he/she variants; markup inside a sentence via `t.rich`. A source scanner (`npm run i18n:extract`) reads every key, and a test fails if any key lacks Arabic or any placeholder differs. Only the keys client components use are sent to the browser (`i18n/client-keys.json`).
- **Names:** fighters, trainers, gyms, events, venues, cities, nicknames and belt names are Latin in every feed, so their Arabic forms live in a `name_translations` table keyed by the English spelling (survives re-ingest, records source and review state). The 1,847 demo names were transliterated in this session by Claude and are all marked **unreviewed**; given names and surnames were unified across batches (39 inconsistent spellings fixed). `npm run i18n:names -- export|import|auto|review`. Search matches Arabic spellings (alef/hamza/yeh/teh-marbuta folded, vowel marks ignored); the rule-based natural-language parser understands common Arabic phrases (divisions, stance, "undefeated", "N knockouts", countries) and with an API key Claude parses any wording.

## 12. Money data and the research pipeline (round 6, 2026-10-03)

**Data model.** Four new optional provider feeds (`fetchFinancials`, `fetchPurses`, `fetchBroadcasts`, `fetchEarnings`; contract in `lib/providers/index.ts`) and tables `event_financials`, `purses`, `event_broadcasts`, `earnings`: gate, tickets, capacity, site fee, PPV buys/price/revenue, sponsorship; one purse per fighter per bout (guaranteed, bonus, total); broadcaster, platform, region, average and peak audience per card; yearly earnings (ring and off-ring). **Every row carries a basis** (`disclosed` official record / `reported` named outlet / `estimated`), a named source, an optional URL and a reading date, and several sources can coexist for one figure: the strongest wins per field and each figure shows its own source (`lib/money.ts`). Validation (`lib/validate-money.ts`) drops rows with no basis or source, bad references, negative amounts or duplicate sources, and flags (keeps) implausible figures: tickets over capacity, PPV revenue that disagrees with buys × price, a purse below its guarantee, a peak below the average. `lib/ingest-money.ts` writes rows per source (re-runs never duplicate) and can add money to a database that already has its fighters and cards.

**Pages.** `/money` (biggest gates, PPV leaders, highest purses, top earners overall and for the year, broadcasters with average audiences, gate+PPV by year, coverage line); a "The money" panel on every event (each figure with its basis chip, linking to its source) and an Earnings card on every fighter; money coverage rows on the Data page. All bilingual. The demo league has simulated money (`lib/providers/demo-money.ts`, its own random stream, marked "simulated" in the source names, league output byte-identical).

**Research pipeline** (`docs/research.md`, `lib/research/`, `npm run research`). Researchers (Claude agents browsing the web, or the extraction bot) only write *claims*: a figure, the page URL and a word-for-word quote. Code then re-fetches every page (`PoliteFetcher`: identifies itself via `RESEARCH_CONTACT`, obeys robots.txt and crawl-delay, 3 s per host, never touches BoxRec, never retries or works round a 403/429/challenge), requires the quote to be on the page and every number to be in the quote, and publishes only figures that two independent sites (one not Wikipedia) agree on within 5%, or one official `.gov` record; a lone source is held (`single_source`), disagreeing sources are a `conflict`. `promote` matches verified claims to bouts by date and both boxers' names and refuses to guess; `apply` writes them through the quality gate. The extraction bot (`research extract`, needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`) drops any fact whose quote is not on the page before the checker sees it; only its logic is tested (mock model), not live. 

**First harvest (run today).** Six agents researched about 35 marquee fights from 1987 to 2025 plus 2015-2025 earnings lists: 427 claims. The code re-check result: **267 verified, 110 single source, 18 conflicts, 32 unconfirmed** (10 of those because ESPN's and Fight News's robots.txt disallow the page, which the agents' curl ignored and the pipeline correctly refused; the rest quote mismatches or paywalled/blocked pages). - **These facts are about real fights and cannot be shown yet**: the app's database holds the fictional league, and `promote` only attaches claims to fighters and cards it finds. They go live when a real feed is loaded. (Showing real money for fictional fighters would be nonsense, and the site must not mix the two.)
- **Source survey** (`docs/data-sources-survey.md`, from a seventh agent, desk research with evidence tags): Wikidata is under-used (19.6k boxers; 392 Hall of Fame IDs, 5.8k Olympedia IDs, 2.8k awards, ~1.9k dated events, no attendance); FightFax (the Association of Boxing Commissions' record keeper, ~1M bouts, sells a licence) is not on our vendor shortlist and is worth a quote; every large open boxing dataset it could trace is a BoxRec scrape and must be avoided (a CC0 label does not clean it); CompuBox, The Ring and Box-Rank forbid automated extraction; commission PDFs (Nevada, Texas) must be fetched by hand.

## 13. Matchmaking and title lineage (round 7, 2026-10-03)

**Title lineage** (`lib/lineage.ts`, `/titles`, `/titles/[belt]`, a Titles card on fighter profiles). A belt is one sanctioning body + title name + division + sex (so an interim belt is its own line). Walking its title fights in date order: the first winner on record is champion; a vacant-title fight crowns its winner (and ends any reign still running as "vacated"); the champion beating a challenger is a defence, losing hands the belt over; a draw keeps it (counted); a no-contest, cancelled or upcoming bout changes nothing; a title fight the champion is not in passes the belt to the winner ("inherited"). It shows reign length, defences, who beat whom, a through-time strip of every reign (gaps = vacant), longest reign, most defences, most reigns, and marks a belt **dormant** when its holder is inactive or no title fight was held for 18 months. What results cannot show (stripped, vacated by retirement, belts passed with no fight) is not guessed. Verified on a hand-built feed covering every transition, and on the demo.
- **The demo's belts were incoherent** (title labels on random main events), which made every lineage a chain of strangers. `lib/providers/demo-belts.ts` now relabels title fights in its own pass after every result exists: a champion's fights in their division are for the belt, a belt left vacant (never held, or dormant for 18 months) is fought over by two proven fighters, a champion who loses hands it over. Results are untouched: the pre-change generator with its title labels removed hashes identically to the current league (`tests/money.test.ts`). Also fixed: re-ingesting never updated a bout's `title`.

**Matchmaking** (`lib/matchmaking.ts`, `/matchmaking`, a "who should he fight next" card on profiles, Open-in-dream-fight links). A pairing's **fight score (0-100)** is a weighted sum of six parts, each 0-1: *competitive* (how close the model has it), *relevance* (rating gap, both top-10), *style* (archetype clash, finishing power, star rating), *availability* (rested but not rusty; retired = 0), *novelty* (never met > a rematch of a close fight > a repeat) and *stakes* (a unification or a champion against a top-5 contender, from the lineage). Weights 0.28/0.18/0.14/0.14/0.12/0.14, written in the code and tested to sum to 1. Every score comes with plain-language reasons. Three views: **fights to make now** (the best pairing among each division's top eight available fighters, strongest eight overall), **who should he fight next** (active, unbooked, same sex, same division or one either side, not training partners, within 260 rating points), and the **dream-fight builder** (any two fighters: model odds, catchweight midway between the divisions' limits, reasons it may never happen such as different sex or divisions, retired, careers that did not overlap, already booked, reach gap, plus their meetings and common opponents with each one's result). All bilingual, reasons included.
- Honest limits: the weights are judgment, not fitted to anything (there is no outcome to fit a "good fight" against; a future version could learn from box-office and viewing data once real money data is loaded); a champion is rated so far above everyone that "competitive" is near zero for most opponents, so their list leans on relevance and stakes; availability is a proxy (time since last fight, no booking), not contract knowledge.

## 17. Venues from Wikidata (round 9, 2026-10-03)

The survey (§12 item 1) listed "venues and dated events" as a Wikidata import. Checked live before building: **it does not work for professional boxing.** Wikidata's boxing events are 427 "sporting events", 278 Olympic events, 201 multi-sport-Games events and 119 "boxing competition" items, i.e. amateur championships and Games located at *city* level ("1955 European Amateur Boxing Championships, Berlin"). There are no structured pro fight cards, and no venues attached to them. What Wikidata does have is good data on the *arenas themselves* (coordinates, capacity), so the venue names on our own events are resolved to it instead.

- **Resolver** (`lib/importers/venues.ts`, `npm run venues:resolve [-- --limit N]`, dry run: `-- --name "Wembley Stadium" --city London --country "United Kingdom"`; needs `WIKIMEDIA_CONTACT`). For each distinct (venue, city) on our events, busiest first: search Wikidata by name, one SPARQL call on the candidates, then the decision in `decide()`. Accepted only if (1) the candidate is a stadium, arena, sports venue, convention centre, casino, theatre or event venue; (2) its label or an alias equals our venue name (case, accents, punctuation and a leading "the" ignored) and exactly one candidate does; (3) it is in our city (anywhere up its "located in" chain) or, failing that, in our country (Wikidata files T-Mobile Arena under the district Paradise, not Las Vegas; the match records which basis it used). If several share the name, the one whose *label* (not alias) is ours wins, which resolves "The O2 Arena" against the "The O2" district whose alias is "O2 Arena". Otherwise the result is `ambiguous` or `no_match` with the reason, and nothing is shown. Misses are retried after 45 days. Capacity is the median of Wikidata's best-ranked values (a general figure, not the boxing configuration), and values outside 50 to 400,000 and impossible coordinates are dropped.
- **Data and pages:** `venues (name, city, country, status, reason, wikidata_id, label, lat, lon, capacity, basis, checked_at)`; `w.venueOf(event)` returns only matched venues. The event page shows "Seats about N (general figure) · Map · Venue data: Wikidata" and the event's structured data gains `sameAs`, `geo` and `maximumAttendeeCapacity` on its `Place`. ## 20. Fight of the year and all-time lists (round 10, 2026-10-03)

**Fight of the year** (`lib/fight-score.ts`, `/fight-of-the-year`, `/fight-of-the-year/[year]`, a score chip on every bout page, a card on the home page, awards chips on fighter profiles). Every completed fight of six rounds or more (no DQ, no-contest, cancelled) gets a **fight score 0-100**: seven parts, each 0-1, with fixed weights: knockdowns 17% (both fighters going down counts more), finish 20% (a split decision or draw is the closest finish; a stoppage late in the distance beats an early one), action 20% (punches landed per round against every fight with statistics, plus how evenly they traded), matchup 16% (evenly matched, both highly rated), upset 9%, stakes 8% (a world title, a vacant one, count most), comeback 10% (the winner was dropped). The highest score of each year wins.
- **Missing data never counts against a fight:** a part that cannot be worked out (no scorecards on a decision, no punch statistics) is dropped and the rest are scaled up; `coverage` and a dash on the part bars say so. Only about 44% of the demo's fights have punch statistics.
- **Every score is explained:** plain-language reasons (only parts that actually helped), the seven bars with their weights, and a tier word (classic 70+, great 55+, good 40+, solid 25+; a median fight scores about 25).
- **Honest limits:** there is no panel and no fan vote, so the score is a judgment, not a consensus; the weights are ours and were not fitted to anything. Title fights tend to win because they are long, rated and staked. The demo's fights are simulated, with few multi-knockdown fights, so its best fight of a year scores about 55-60.

**All-time lists** (`lib/records.ts`, `/all-time`, `/all-time/[list]`). Sixteen lists in three groups, each with a published definition and a sex and division filter (a plain GET form, so every view has an address): fighters (greatest of all time by legacy score, highest peak rating, most wins, most knockouts, highest KO rate with 15+ wins, longest winning run, most wins over opponents rated 1600+); titles (most title-fight wins, most defences, longest reigns, most defences in one reign, champions in the most divisions); fights (greatest fights by fight score, biggest upsets, fastest knockouts, most knockdowns).
- **Legacy score:** peak rating 30%, wins over 1600+ opponents 25% (capped at 8), reign time 15% (capped at 8 years), defences 10% (capped at 15), longest run 10% (capped at 30), honours 10%. The honours part is left out for everyone when the data has no honours at all (the demo has none). The parts are shown out of 100 on every row. The caps and weights are judgment.
- **"All-time" means the data:** every page says it covers the fights in the feed (7,496 bouts, 2014-2026 in the demo); earlier careers are not counted.
- **Dormant belts:** a reign still "running" on a belt whose champion is inactive and with no title fight in 18 months stops at that belt's last title fight instead of counting to today.
- Money records stay on `/money` (linked), where each figure carries its source.

**Plumbing:** `World.punchTotals()` reads whole-bout punch totals lazily (round 0 is a whole-fight total when a feed has it, otherwise rounds are added), so the world build is not slower. Sitemap, ⌘K, nav ("All-time"), Arabic (129 new strings, machine-translated), the header nav now fits at 1280 px (padding) and the header search moved to 1700 px (it overflowed at 1536 px and wider before). Tests: `tests/awards.test.ts` (fights hand-built so each score is worked out by hand) and `tests/records.test.ts` (a draw not breaking a run, dormant belts); every rule was broken on purpose to confirm a test fails.

## 21. Guarding the AI bill (round 9, 2026-10-03)

Found by reading every path that reaches the Anthropic API from anonymous traffic: (1) the plain-English search on `/boxers?q=` called the model for **every distinct query with no cache**, so anyone could run up the bill (or exhaust the key's rate limit) by searching for anything; (2) scouting reports and previews were cached per fighter or bout, day and language, but in a `Map` that was never trimmed, so it grew for as long as the server stayed up; (3) nothing limited how many different fighters or bouts one visitor could ask about.

- **Limits** (`lib/ai-guard.ts`): a per-client limit (`AI_CLIENT_LIMIT`, default 20 model calls per `AI_CLIENT_WINDOW_MS`, default 10 minutes) and a **daily budget for the whole site** (`AI_DAILY_BUDGET`, default 1,000 per UTC day; 0 turns the model off). Only a call that would really reach the model counts; cache hits are free. The client id is the first `X-Forwarded-For` hop, then `X-Real-IP`, else one shared bucket. That header can be spoofed by anyone who can reach the server directly, which is why the daily budget, not the per-client limit, is the hard stop. State is in memory per process: behind several instances each has its own budget.
- **A refused call is not an error:** search, reports and previews all fall back to the rule-based answer, which is always complete. That answer is **not cached**, so one visitor's refusal never stops the next from getting the model's.
- **Search** (`parseQuery`): input cut to 200 characters; results cached per day and normalised query (case and spacing ignored) so a repeat costs nothing; concurrent identical searches share one call.
- **Caches** are now bounded LRUs of 2,000 entries (`lib/lru.ts`), so they no longer grow without limit.
- **Tests** (`tests/ai-guard.test.ts`, mocked model): window and expiry, other clients unaffected, daily hard stop that rotating ids cannot get around, reset on a new UTC day, budget 0, bounded client table, client id parsing, LRU order, the search cases above, refusals not remembered for reports and previews, and the routes using the caller's address. Removing the budget check, the in-flight sharing, or the "do not cache a refusal" rule each fails tests.
- **Limits of this change:** it bounds the cost, it does not meter it in money (a call's cost depends on the model chosen with `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`); no CAPTCHA or authentication, and the other public endpoints (`/api/search`, `/api/fighters`, `/api/watch`) are cheap and unmetered; the budget resets when the process restarts, so a crash-looping server would let more calls through. Not covered here: the build-time scripts (`i18n:translate`, `research extract`), which are run by a person with their own key.

## 22. Model accountability: the track record (round 10, 2026-10-03)

The first of the AI bets: a model that publishes its own report card. Everything on the page is computed from results already in the database, so there is nothing for an AI to invent, and every number can be reproduced. (AI is for narrating and querying this later; the figures stay code.)

- **What it is** (`lib/accountability.ts`, `/accountability`): for every completed, decisive bout, the model's win probability as it would have stood *before* the fight, using the same `winProbability` and active weights as the predictor page: pre-fight Elo (`boutPre`), each fighter's record and layoff up to their previous bout, age that year. Fighters with no earlier bout in the database are skipped, and the set of scored bouts is **identical to the fitter's** (`buildDataset`), pinned by a test. Draws and no-contests are not scored.
- **It says what it is:** a **backtest, not a live record**. The settings were chosen with these results in view, so the headline uses the most recent 25% of bouts (the period the fitter held out), and the page states which weights are in use (hand-set, or Elo scale fitted).
- **What it shows:** fights scored; pick accuracy against the higher-rated-fighter baseline and a coin flip; log loss against plain Elo and a coin flip; a **reliability diagram and table** (when the model says 70%, is it right 70% of the time?) with a written verdict (calibrated / under-confident / over-confident, bins under 20 fights ignored, 3-point tolerance); predicted against actual early finishes; accuracy by year and by division; the ten biggest surprises by the model's own numbers; how it is computed. Completed bout pages gain "The model's call before the fight" (and "toss-up" wording when it saw no clear favourite). Linked from Analytics, the sitemap and ⌘K. English and Arabic (machine translation, unreviewed; the table's ranges are forced left-to-right so "50-60%" does not flip).
- **What it found on the demo league (fitted weights):** the win probabilities are well calibrated (pick accuracy 68.6% vs 68.0% for "higher rating wins", log loss 0.581 vs 0.617 for plain Elo). With the hand-set defaults (no `data/model-fit.json`) the same page reports the model as **under-confident**: at 60-70% confidence it picked the winner 78% of the time. **The early-finish estimate is off in both cases: the model expects about 55% of fights to end inside the distance and about 40% do.** The previews' "how it could end" split inherits that, so the finish heuristic in `lib/model.ts` should be re-fitted on real data (`npm run model:fit` does not cover it yet). Publishing this gap is the point of the page. **Update (§29):** the finish estimate is now fitted by `npm run model:fit` and the page reports it calibrated on the demo league.
- **Tests** (`tests/accountability.test.ts`, `tests/accountability-leak.test.ts`): same fights as the fitter; with pure-Elo weights a call equals the Elo expectation; hand-computed accuracy, log loss, Brier, baselines, bins and finish figures; verdict thresholds; the record's headline period, upsets ordering and division threshold. **No leakage:** a small league is ingested, then re-ingested with the last result flipped, and every earlier call (and the last fight's own prediction) is unchanged; flipping an early result does change later calls (so the check is not vacuous); a result turned into a draw moves nothing before it. Scoring against final ratings, or after updating the state with the fight's own result, each fails the suite.
- **Not done / next:** a **live ledger** (predictions locked before a card and graded after, which cannot be replayed afterwards), users' picks against the model (needs accounts to be a leaderboard; local-only is possible now), an automatic post-fight recap (surprise, ranking and title impact), and a nav entry (the bar is full, so the page is linked from Analytics and ⌘K for now). **Update:** the live ledger (§24), My picks (§25), the post-fight recap (§26) and the nav entry (§23) have since been built; a server-side leaderboard is still open.

## 24. The live ledger (round 10, 2026-10-03)

The backtest on the Track record page can be flattered by hindsight however carefully it is built, because the model's settings were chosen with those results in view. A ledger cannot be: each prediction is written down while the fight is still in the future and is never edited.

- **How it works** (`lib/ledger.ts`, table `prediction_snapshots`): whenever the world is built (server start, every new day, every data change, in any process), today's snapshot is appended for each upcoming, not-cancelled bout that has none yet (`INSERT OR IGNORE`, one row per bout per day). A long build-up is therefore recorded as it drifts with ratings and layoffs. A row holds the model's decisive-conditional win probability for red, the draw probability, the finish probability, the plain-Elo baseline, **both fighters' inputs and the weights used**, a fingerprint of those weights (`model`) and real and app dates. A failure to write (a locked database, an old file without the table) is swallowed so no page ever breaks.
- **Grading:** a bout is graded on its **last snapshot taken strictly before the event date**, the model's final word. Snapshots dated on or after the event are ignored, so nothing can be added in time to look right. A fight that first appeared already decided (or whose first snapshot is dated after it) is **never claimed**; draws, no-contests and cancellations are counted as voided, not scored. Results are read live from the bouts table, so a corrected result corrects the grade and never the prediction.
- **Where it shows:** a Live ledger section at the top of `/accountability` (fights on file and since when, fights awaiting, graded count with accuracy against "higher rating wins", a note until 30 fights are graded, the latest graded calls with right/wrong, and the next fights on file with their probabilities), and a chip on each upcoming bout page ("Prediction on file since {date}: 47% / 53%"). English and Arabic.
- **On the demo league** the ledger starts empty and fills on first run (46 upcoming bouts on file); nothing can be graded until the demo's upcoming events happen, and the demo has no mechanism to play them, so the graded part is exercised by tests (and by the real feed once it is loaded). That is also why the headline numbers stay the backtest's for now.
- **Tests** (`tests/ledger.test.ts`, a hand-made league and calendar, moving the app's clock): one snapshot per bout per day however often the world rebuilds; a new day appends and keeps the old; the cancelled bout is not predicted; a snapshot equals the live model's number and carries inputs and fingerprint; grading uses the last snapshot before the event and ignores forged rows dated on or after it; changing the result or the ratings later never alters an earlier row; the same prediction with a different result changes only the grade; history and late snapshots are not claimed; draws are voided; pending list order; a missing table cannot stop the world from building. Grading on the newest snapshot regardless of date fails the suite.
- **Not done / limits:** the ledger only records while a process is running the world (no server, no snapshots; a day with no run leaves a gap, and the grade then uses the last earlier snapshot); it is per database file, so a deployment must keep that file; users' picks against the model, a post-fight recap and a leaderboard are still to build; with several server instances writing to one SQLite file a snapshot can occasionally be skipped (retried at the next rebuild). **Update:** users' picks (§25) and the post-fight recap (§26) have since been built; the leaderboard is still open.

## 26. The post-fight recap: what the result changed (round 10, 2026-10-03)

## 27. Upset watch and trainer impact (round 12, 2026-10-03)

**Upset watch** (`lib/upsets.ts`, `/upset-watch`, `/feeds/upset-watch.xml`, a card on the home page, a chip on live-underdog fights in `/previews`). Every upcoming fight, ranked by the underdog's chance of winning from the same model as the predictor (one number for a fight everywhere on the site). Tiers: too close to call (40%+), live underdog (28-40%), longshot (under 28%). Each fight lists the model's own factors that favour the underdog, plus warning signs about the favourite the model does not use: a long layoff (15+ months), age 36+, coming off a stoppage loss or a loss, a new head trainer, missed weights, a weak chin against a finisher, and an underdog on a 4+ fight run. An Atom feed of the live underdogs is the "alert" (no accounts needed); `?lang=ar` for Arabic.
- **It checks itself.** The track-record table compares, tier by tier, how often past underdogs won with how often the model said they would (point-in-time probabilities from `accountability.calls`, and the last quarter of fights as the held-out stretch). On the demo the tiers agree within about 1 point except longshots, who win about 3 points more often than the model says.
- **The warning signs are tested, not assumed** (`signalLift`): a long layoff, age 36+ and a stoppage loss last time show no effect beyond the model; a loss last time and especially an underdog on a 4+ fight winning run do (46% against 39% predicted, n=254, about 2.6 standard errors). The page states a verdict only when the gap is about twice its margin of error and says the other signs (new trainer, missed weights, chin) cannot be rebuilt as of fight night, so they are context, not proven edges. These findings are about the simulated league; real data may differ, and the page recomputes them.

**Trainer impact** (`lib/trainer-impact.ts`, `/trainers`, an "Impact on results" section on each head trainer's page, a link from the people page). "Their fighters' ratings went up" is biased (young fighters rise whoever trains them), so every completed bout is modelled as `level(red) + effect(red's trainer) − level(blue) − effect(blue's trainer)`, fitted together with a ridge penalty, in Elo points against the average trainer. Only fighters who changed trainer separate a trainer's effect from their fighters' ability, so evidence is counted from those fights and trainers with too little are marked thin and never ranked.
- **Validated against a known truth:** the demo generator gives each trainer a hidden boost (`demoTruth()`), and the fitted effects correlate with it at about 0.4 (0.53 with a firmer fighter penalty; the weaker penalty is chosen on purpose because real fighters are not assigned to trainers at random and a firm penalty would credit a trainer with their fighters' average strength). The 95% ranges hold the truth for about 94% of trainers. A synthetic league with a known truth (tests) shows the fit recovers effects when fighters move (r above 0.6) and claims little when they do not.
- **The result is mostly "cannot tell".** On the demo only about 1 of 150 trainers is clearly above or below average and about 10 more lean one way; the ranges are about ±85 Elo wide. The page says so up top and shows the range for every row instead of a bare ranking.
- **Does changing trainer matter?** A plain comparison: rating change over the next six fights for fighters who changed head trainer (+4.9) against fighters who stayed (−0.7): +5.6 ± 1.9 Elo. Real but small, and not causal (fighters switch after bad runs).
- **Underdog lifters** (wins against Elo expectation when under 40%) is shown as a list to watch: at these sample sizes it is mostly luck, and the page says so.
- **Limits:** a fighter's ability is treated as fixed through a career; the standard error ignores how fighter and trainer effects overlap; the penalty is a judgment (a typical trainer within about 60 Elo); the effect is of the head trainer only.

Both: Arabic (128 strings, machine-translated; number-agreement strings use plural forms), sitemap, ⌘K, nav (Upset watch under Fights, Trainer impact under Camps and money), tests (`tests/upsets.test.ts`, `tests/trainer-impact.test.ts`, `tests/trainer-moves.test.ts`; mutation-checked), axe clean and no overflow at six widths on every new or changed page.

## 28. Ask the data (round 13, 2026-10-03)

**What it is** (`lib/ask/`, `/ask`, `/api/ask`, a link from the home page, ⌘K, nav). A question in plain English or Arabic, answered from the database and never from a model's memory. Three steps:
1. **Plan.** The question becomes up to three calls to a fixed set of read-only tools: `fighters` (filters and sort), `record_list` (the 16 all-time lists), `fighter`, `head_to_head`, `rankings`, `champions`, `bouts` (search completed fights), `events`, `fight_of_the_year`, `upset_watch`, `trainers`, `money`. With an API key Claude picks the tools and arguments; without one, or when it fails or is rate-limited, patterns do (`rules.ts`, English and Arabic). Every call is checked against the tool's declared arguments, so anything a model makes up (an extra argument, an unknown tool, a number out of range) is dropped: it can run these queries and nothing else.
2. **Run.** The tools run over the in-memory world. Their tables are always shown beside the answer, with links, so any answer can be checked.
3. **Answer.** With a key Claude writes two to four sentences from the tool results only; **any figure in its answer that is not in the results throws the answer away** for the rule-based one. Without a key a sentence per tool is used. The page says which wrote it ("Written by AI" or "Put together by rules"), and that no key is set when that is so.

**Cost and abuse.** At most two model calls per question, each counted by `lib/ai-guard.ts` (per-client and daily limits, so a refused call falls back to rules and says so, and is not cached); answers are cached per language and day; two parallel identical questions share one run. The question is capped at 300 characters and the prompts tell the model the question and results are data, not instructions (a test sends an injection and checks that nothing but the tools can be reached). Pages with a question are `noindex`.

**Honest limits.**
- **The AI path has not run against the real API** (no key here): it is tested against a mocked model (prompts, plan validation, grounding, fallbacks, limits, caching), not against Claude's actual behaviour. Expect to tune the prompts on first real use.
- The rule planner covers the question shapes above; anything else gets "I could not match that" rather than a guess. It is the floor, not the product.
- A model can still choose a poor tool for a question; the tables make that visible, and the grounding check stops invented numbers but not a misread question.
- Machine-translated Arabic (91 strings + plural forms) and Arabic patterns are limited to the example shapes and a handful of phrasings.
- Tests (`tests/ask.test.ts`, 17, mutation-checked): plan cleaning, every pattern and example in both languages, each tool against a raw count of the data, grounding, the AI path with a fake model, limits, caching, injection, API.

**Scale (160,000 bouts):** the data work for a question is 7 to 200 ms (the cold all-time list is the slowest); a model call, when there is one, is far longer.

## 29. Fixing the finish estimate the Track record page caught (round 11, 2026-10-03)

§22 published, against the model's own name, that its early-finish estimate was off: it expected about 55% of fights to end by stoppage and about 40% did, and the fight previews' "how it could end" split inherits that. This round fits it.

- **What was wrong:** the estimate was a hand-set rule, `0.22 + 0.35 × (both fighters' KO rates) + 0.2 × (both KO-loss rates)`, clamped to 10-85%. On the demo league's held-out fights it scored **worse than predicting the base rate** (log-loss 0.750 against 0.674) and averaged 55.6% against 40.3% actual. In this league a fighter's past KO rate barely predicts whether *this* fight ends early, so the rule's confident spread (some upcoming fights at 72% to 82%) was not backed by anything.
- **The fit** (`runFinishFit` in `lib/fit.ts`, part of `npm run model:fit`): a logistic regression of "ended by stoppage" on the two fighters' summed KO rates and summed KO-loss rates, trained on the first 75% of bouts and tested on the last 25% (the same bouts and split as the win model, checked by a test). It is recommended only if it beats the hand-set rule by `MIN_GAIN` (0.002) on held-out log-loss, the same parsimony rule as the win model. On the demo league: log-loss 0.6723 (base rate 0.6743), averaging 41.6% against 40.3% actual, coefficients +0.233 per KO rate and +0.091 per KO-loss rate (small: the signal is weak here). It is applied to every finish estimate when recommended.
- **How it is applied:** `FinishModel` and `stoppageProbability(a, b, finish = activeFinish())` in `lib/model.ts`; `applyFittedFinish` in `lib/model-fit.ts` runs with the win-model weights when the world is built, independently of them (a report can fit one and not the other; an older report with no finish section changes nothing). Predictions take the finish model as a parameter, and the matchup lab is handed it as a prop so the browser agrees with the server. `modelVersion()` in the ledger includes the finish model, and is **unchanged while none is fitted**, so existing ledger fingerprints keep their meaning.
- **The Track record page** now says whether the finish estimate is fitted, and after a fit shows the gap closed: predicted 41.6% against 40.3% actual, verdict "calibrated" (before: 55.6%, "runs high").
- **Tests** (`tests/finish-fit.test.ts`): the hand-set rule is exactly what it was when nothing is fitted; a fitted model is a clamped logistic (never 0 or 100%); predictions use the active model, an explicit one overrides it; the fit uses the same bouts and split as the win model, beats the hand-set rule, averages what happened; the recommendation needs a real gain (0.0015 is noise, more than 0.002 counts); applying a report sets, clears or leaves alone correctly; the page's verdict goes from "runs high" to "calibrated"; the fingerprint is stable without a finish model and changes with one. Always recommending the fit, or never clearing a stale one, each fails the suite. Passes with and without a `data/model-fit.json` on disk.
- **What it means for the product:** stoppage language on the site ("Stoppage likely", "Expect a stoppage") now appears only when the data supports it. On the demo league that is almost never, because the simulator does not tie finishing to past KO rate; on a real feed `npm run model:fit` will find whatever relationship exists, and the Track record page will show whether it held.
- **Not done:** more features for the finish estimate (scheduled rounds, division, rating gap, referee and judge tendencies) are plausible but each must earn its place on held-out data; the finish model is not yet shown on the Data page's model report (the CLI output is the report for now).

## 30. Night in review (round 11, 2026-10-03)

The recap says what one fight changed; the night in review says what the whole card did. It sits at the top of every completed event page and is built from the recap of each decided fight on the card (`lib/night.ts`), so it cannot disagree with the bout pages. Facts first, sentences second, in both languages; no key needed.

- **What it reports, most newsworthy first:** new champions and title defences (with the belt in the reader's language); **the night's biggest surprise**, the winner the model gave the lowest chance when that was under 40%; how many fights were decided and how many ended inside the distance; the fastest finish (earliest round, then the clock); how many winners the model picked (its pre-fight call, the same number each bout page shows); the biggest rise within a division's top 15, counting a new entry as a rise from just outside; the longest win streak run up on the night (3+); and unbeaten runs (5+ fights) that ended. - **Tests** (`tests/night.test.ts`, every completed card in the league): decided and early-finish counts, new champions and defences agree with the recaps; the surprise is the card's least likely winner and equals the number on its bout page; the model's record equals the sum of its calls on the card's fights; nothing ended in an earlier round than the "fastest finish"; the climb is a real rise within the top 15 that appears in a bout recap; the streak is the card's longest; both languages complete with no leftover placeholders and no English sentence words in Arabic; a new champion always leads. Picking the most likely winner as the surprise, or the slowest finish as the fastest, each fails the suite. About 100 cards are built in 200 ms.
- **Wording:** names are used instead of pronouns and counts avoid number-noun agreement (Arabic "عدد النزالات المحسومة: 14") so no gendered or mis-agreed forms are possible.

## 32. The smoke check: every page, both languages, on a real server (round 11, 2026-10-03)

About 115 requests, under 10 seconds.
- **What it checks per page:** HTTP 200; `<html lang>` and `dir` match the URL (an English page for an Arabic URL, or Arabic that is not right-to-left, fails); at least one `<h1>`; and in the text a reader sees (scripts and styles stripped) none of: the word `undefined`, `NaN`, `[object Object]`, `Infinity`, `null`, an unfilled `{placeholder}`, or an error page. APIs must return valid JSON, the portrait a real SVG, and an unknown URL a real 404 with `noindex`.
- **It cannot be outgrown:** a test fails if a new `[param]` page is added under `app/[locale]` without a sampler in `lib/smoke.ts`, or if a navigation entry is missing from the route list.
- **Proven, not assumed:** on the demo league all 114 checks pass. With two bugs injected on purpose (a fighter page printing "undefined", and a people page throwing) the check reported exactly those pages in both languages, with the offending text shown; reverting them returned it to 114 of 114.
- **Finding, not fixed (framework behaviour):** for an unknown URL or a fighter, event or bout that does not exist, the server returns the correct **404 status and a `noindex` tag**, but the HTML body is empty; the not-found page ("Not on the card") reaches the visitor only through the streamed payload that the browser renders. A visitor with JavaScript sees the page in both languages (checked in Chromium); one without sees a blank page. Next's streaming documentation describes this class of behaviour. Impact is small (search engines get 404 and noindex; no-JavaScript visitors on a missing page), so it is recorded here and the smoke check asserts what is true: a 404, `noindex`, and the heading present in the response.
- **Not done:** it samples one page per kind rather than every fighter (a data-dependent crash on a rare record would need a bigger sample or the real feed's edge cases); it does not run a browser, so client-only breakage is not seen; with a real data feed the sample should be re-checked against that feed's rarest records.

## 37. The finish estimate learns from the matchup (round 15, 2026-10-03)

§29 fitted the early-finish estimate on two inputs (both fighters' KO rates and KO-loss rates). §29 left open whether anything else earns a place. This round tried each candidate on the same held-out fights and kept what clears the same bar as everywhere else (`|z| >= 2` on the training fights, then the fit must beat the hand-set rule by `MIN_GAIN`).

- **Tried** (added one at a time to the two-input fit, held-out log-loss gain): rating mismatch +0.0031; weight class (light or heavy) +0.0010 to +0.0025; scheduled rounds −0.0017 to −0.0004; title fight −0.0006; women's fights −0.0002. Rounds, title and sex added nothing and are not in the model.
- **Kept:** `mismatch` (the plain-Elo gap before the fight, 0 even to 1 certain; z 3.4, so lopsided fights end early more often) and `weight` (the division's limit, 0 at 150 lb to 1 at 220 lb and no limit, negative below; z 5.1, heavier divisions end early more often). **Dropped:** the KO-loss rate (z 0.2 once the others are in; its coefficient is exactly 0). The KO rate stays (z 2.9).
- **Result on the demo league (held-out, 1,587 fights):** log-loss 0.6681 against 0.6723 for KO rates alone, 0.6743 for the base rate and 0.7501 for the hand-set rule; the average estimate is 42.2% against 40.3% that happened. The finish estimate is still weak: it separates fights a little, it does not call them.
- **Where the inputs come from.** `Features.weightLb` is set from the fighter's division (`featuresOf`) and, in the backtest, from the division of **that fight** (a fighter who moves up was not at the new weight before). Two classes in one fight: the heavier limit. Unknown weight counts as mid-range. The ratings are those before the fight. No call signature changed.
- **Old reports still work:** a `model-fit.json` from before this applies with the new terms at zero. `npm run model:fit` prints each input's z, whether it was kept, and the KO-rates-only comparison.
- **Honest limits.** The demo league is synthetic: its generator may build these effects in, so the *sizes* above are not evidence about real boxing; the *procedure* (propose, test on later fights, keep only what earns its place) is what carries over. Mismatch and weight class are the obvious candidates; referee and judge tendencies, scheduled rounds against real round-by-round data and styles are still untried. The fit is not yet shown on the Track record page beyond "fitted to results".

## 45. The backfill runbook: resumable, priced before it is spent (round 23, 2026-10-03)

**Why.** The adapter (§38) can map a feed; it could not yet *run* one: a full history is thousands of requests, a failure partway lost everything, and nothing kept the league current. This is the procedure and the tooling for the day a plan with full history is bought. As first written, nothing here ran until the vendor confirmed storage in writing; **by the owner's decision the default is now to store provisionally** (see the update below), and none of it has run against the real API.

**What was built.**
- **`npm run vendor:backfill`**, four modes: `--plan` (fetch the fight list in memory and say what the fighters will cost; writes nothing, needs no confirmation), `--check` (fetch into the cache, validate, print the report, touch nothing), the load (fetch, validate strictly, back up, load in one transaction, recompute ratings) and `--update` (the daily job).
- **A read-through cache** of every successful response (`lib/providers/boxing-data-api.ts`, `cacheDir`): a crashed or interrupted run costs only the requests that never completed; a finished one costs none, so a mapping fix is re-run for free. Only a good answer is kept (a refusal or an error body never is), writes are atomic, and a damaged file is fetched again.
- **Retries** with Retry-After or 1, 2, 4 ... 30 s back-off on a 429, a 500/502/503/504 or a dropped connection; a refusal (403) is never retried; a retry spends the request budget like any request.
- **Safety refusals:** the storage confirmation is checked before anything is created; a database that holds other fighters (the demo league) is refused unless `--into-existing`; a load with any fighter missing is refused (re-run to retry only those); a validator error stops the load; a backup is taken before any load into a database with data (the last 14 kept).
- **The app no longer starts a paid fetch on a page view.** An empty database with `BOXING_PROVIDER=licensed` now refuses to seed and names `vendor:backfill` (`lib/db.ts`). Before, the first request (or the health check) would have started the fetch.
- **The daily update** asks for the fights since the latest card in the database (less 14 days), fetches everything in its window fresh (a cached list page or career record predates today's results), and replaces "no result yet" with the result; a running app notices the change itself (its world rebuilds when another process commits).
- `docs/real-data-runbook.md`: the ordered procedure, the checks to make by hand, the cron line, what each error means, undoing it, and what is stored where.

**Tests** (all new, 3 files): `tests/boxing-data-api.test.ts` grew to 33 (resume, cache integrity, retries and back-off, the plan); `tests/vendor-backfill.test.ts` (3: the foreign-database check, the update window, the plan text); `tests/db-licensed-guard.test.ts` (2); and `tests/vendor-backfill-cli.test.ts` (11) **runs the real command end to end against a local HTTP server standing in for the vendor**, with a real database file: plan, refusal, check, load, reload, the wrong-database guard and backup, a failed fighter and the resume, the daily update picking up a late result, an update on an empty database, and a wrong key. Mutation-checked: an ignored cache, retried refusals, an uncapped wait, free retries, cached error bodies, the foreign-database guard, a late confirmation check, cached lists on update, an allowed incomplete load, and no empty-database guard each fail a test. The end-to-end test found two real flaws while it was being written (an empty database file left behind by a refused run; two backups in the same second colliding).

**Honest limits.** Not run against the real API. The plan prints a time from a guessed 250 ms round trip. The cache is the vendor's data on disk, which the storage terms must allow. A daily update costs one request per fighter in its window. A feed whose history is shallow gives shallow ratings whatever the tooling does.

**Update, same day: storing is on by default (the owner's decision).** The vendor has not answered the question of whether a stored backfill may be kept, and the owner chose to go ahead provisionally rather than wait. So `BOXING_API_STORAGE_CONFIRMED` is no longer a gate: **unset means store, with a warning on every run** ("storing PROVISIONALLY ... if it says no, delete the cache and the database"); `1` records that the vendor agreed in writing and silences the warning; `0` refuses to store (a sample can still be evaluated). Nothing else was loosened: the budget, the wrong-database refusal, the completeness gate, the validator gate, the backup and the empty-database guard are unchanged. The real risk is the vendor's terms, not the code: if it says no, the vendor's data must be deleted, which is why the real league has its own database file and cache directory (`docs/real-data-runbook.md`, "Undoing it"), and why the cache and backups are gitignored.

**Update, same day: what the load can and cannot promise.** Asked to store only data that could be confidently confirmed as correct. The honest answer is that no code can confirm a vendor's facts; it can check whether the feed agrees with itself. The vendor gives each fighter's career record (`stats`), the adapter now keeps it (`vendorRecords()`), and `lib/vendor-verify.ts` compares it with the loaded fights: **complete** (they add up exactly), **partial** (fights missing: the site would show a shorter record than the fighter has, a false statement), **conflict** (more than the vendor's own total: the feed contradicts itself). A load is refused unless 90% of fighters are complete and none conflict (`--min-complete`, `--allow-partial`, `--allow-conflicts`); `--check` prints the same and exits 1 if a load would be refused; the daily update audits the careers in the database afterwards. This makes the free plan unloadable as a league (its window of a few weeks leaves nearly everyone partial), which is correct. The audit also caught a real flaw in the update on its first run: cached fighter records predate a fight, so every fighter who had just fought looked like a contradiction. The update now fetches its window fresh. Tests: `tests/vendor-verify.test.ts` (4) and two end-to-end scenarios in `tests/vendor-backfill-cli.test.ts` (a short career refused and then loaded on a lower bar; a contradiction refused even with `--allow-partial`). Mutation-checked: a disabled gate, an update that reuses the cache, `--allow-partial` allowing conflicts, and a conflict classed as partial each fail a test. What this still cannot do: say a winner, a date or a method is right. Two systems agreeing is evidence, not proof.

## 47. When something breaks: error pages, an error log, and an Arabic bug found on the way (round 25, 2026-10-03)

## 48. A career total that trails yesterday's result (round 24, 2026-10-03)

## 51. A configuration doctor, and a test that keeps the settings documented (round 28, 2026-10-03)
After the code, the next thing that fails a deployment is the settings around it: a misspelt name that is silently ignored, a `SITE_URL` that is `http://`, a backup that stopped running last week, an `accounts.db` anyone on the machine can read. This round adds a way to ask "will this do what I think it will?" before the first visitor, and fixes a drift between what the code reads and what the files say.
- **`npm run doctor`** (`lib/doctor.ts`, `scripts/doctor.ts`): some fifty distinct findings in two layers, settings only (`envFindings`) and settings plus the files they point to (`fileFindings`, through an injectable `Probe` so every disk state is a unit test, not a staged directory). Each finding is `fail` / `warn` / `info` / `ok` with the fix beside it; exit 1 on any failure, `--strict` for warnings, `--json`, `--production` (the shell you run it from rarely has `NODE_ENV=production`). Never prints a secret: key findings say missing / short / odd, and the log line carries only a `SITE_URL`'s origin (a password pasted into the URL cannot reach a log; tested).
- **Start-up log:** one `{"event":"config"}` JSON line and one `config_problem` line per setting problem, from `instrumentation.ts`. Settings only, no disk, so no start-up cost.
- **Misspelt names** are matched case-insensitively within edit distance 2 of a setting the code reads ("Did you mean SITE_URL?"). My first draft warned about any unknown variable with a familiar prefix and flagged `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` and `AI_AGENT` (other tools' variables) on the first run on this machine; a doctor that cries wolf gets ignored, so it reports near misses only.
- **The drift this found:** `tests/config-docs.test.ts` scans the source for every setting read (`process.env.X`, `process.env["X"]`, `envNum("X")`) and requires it to be in `KNOWN_ENV` (or the short `INTERNAL_ENV` list of test hooks), in `.env.example`, and read by something (no stale entries). Seven settings were read by code and absent from `.env.example`: four public ones now added (`DESIGN_LAB`, `VENDOR_LAG_DAYS`, `WIKIMEDIA_GAP_MS`, `WIKIDATA_GAP_MS`) and three marked internal (`I18N_DIR`, `REVIEW_OUT`, `RINGSIDE_NO_SEED`). (My own audit before writing the test said fourteen; seven of those were already documented. The test, not my grep, is the reliable count.) The test's first run also found a problem the audit had not: `BOXING_API_STORAGE_CONFIRMED` was described in `.env.example` without an `=`, so it could not be uncommented as a line. It also checks that copying `.env.example` turns nothing on (the only live values are an empty key and `BOXING_PROVIDER=demo`) and that the docs name no setting one letter away from a real one.
- **Checked:** type check, lint, 21 new tests (every threshold at its boundary: Node 22.4 vs 22.5, backup 47 vs 49 hours, disk 99 vs 100 vs 499 vs 500 MB, accounts file mode 600/640/644/700; and the CLI end to end on real SQLite files: good, loose permissions, `--strict`, a text file that is not a database, a foreign database). Ten deliberate breaks of the code each failed a test; the one that survived was a guard for platform variables that could never match anything, which I deleted rather than keep.
- **Not done / limits:** the doctor reads the process's environment and the files; it cannot tell whether a key is *valid* (that needs a network call to the vendor, deliberately not made) or whether the volume is really persistent (a container that loses `/data` looks like a fresh install). It does not run on a schedule or alert; wire `npm run doctor -- --production --strict` into the deploy script or a cron if you want that.

## 54. Evidence that is a file (round 25, 2026-10-03)

- **The gap:** the research checker confirmed a claim by fetching its web page and finding the quote. A purse report that arrives by email after a public-records request has no page, so the best sources (commission documents) could not reach "verified" by code.
- **What was built** (`lib/research/documents.ts`, `docs/research.md`): a person registers a document once (`npm run research -- add-document`): its SHA-256, who issued it, when and how it was received, and whether it is an official record. A claim then says `"document": "name"` instead of a URL, and `check` reads the document's text (a PDF through `pdftotext`) and requires the quote and its numbers, with no network and no contact needed. A document that is unregistered, missing or **changed since registration** makes the claim `unconfirmed`.
- **Who can say "official":** only the person registering it. A claim cannot declare its own evidence official; a transcription (typed from a scan) can never be official, even if the manifest is hand-edited to say so (found by a mutation that survived the first tests: registration refused it, but the checker's own guard had no test). Promotion keeps the document's own basis (`disclosed` only if registered official, else `reported`) and puts its name and hash prefix in the money row's note; no web address is invented.
- **What it proves and does not:** the quote is in the document the owner registered and the document is unchanged. Not that the document is genuine: that rests on the person who got it from the issuer.

## 66. A search that forgives a slip, and an index that was rebuilt on every keystroke (round 35, 2026-10-03)
Everything about finding a fighter starts with typing a name, and the search was exact substring matching: every word typed had to appear in a name. Accents, word order and half-typed names were fine; one wrong or missing letter found nothing. I measured it first with a battery of 3,439 queries made from the league's own names (`tests/search-battery.ts`, `npm run search:battery`): letters missing, wrong, swapped, doubled; vowels confused; initials with and without a full stop; a hyphen dropped; nicknames; shouted queries; stray spaces and punctuation; and Arabic misspellings. The fighter meant had to be among the eight results the type-ahead shows.
- **Before, on main (in the top eight):** group A (the mistakes I designed for) 48%, B (related ones) 1%, D (kinds written after the matcher) 14%, Arabic 75%, C (respellings by ear) 0%. Every kind of one-letter slip scored 0%, and "T. Villalba" failed 86% of the time because of the full stop.
- **After:** A 100% (first result 92%), B 99% (95%), D 100% (96%), Arabic 98% (96%), C 98% (94%). Exact search is unchanged: a name typed as written is first 98+% of the time and an exact match is never padded with near ones.
- **How it works** (`lib/fuzzy.ts`, `lib/fighter-search.ts`): when nothing matches exactly, look for names with a word close to each word typed: starting with it, or one slip away (a letter missing, added or wrong, or two neighbours swapped), or one slip from the start of it (for a half-typed word). Three letters or fewer must match exactly (too many words are one slip apart), one or two letters are an initial and must start a word, four to six letters get one slip, seven or more get two. The scan runs over the distinct words of all names (with neighbouring words run together, so "Alqahtani", "Al Qahtani" and "Al-Qahtani" are one name), not over the fighters; the fighter with the fewest slips overall comes first, then the more experienced.
- **Honest accounting.** Group D was written after the first version of the matcher and measured once before it was touched again: **99% in the top eight and 95% first**. That measurement showed punctuation (`!`, `?`, quotes) was not a separator, and group B showed the number of slips allowed followed the typed word's length instead of the name's (two letters missing from a long name); I fixed both, so the final D and B numbers include fixes I made after seeing them. A later check on real queries found my long-name rule too generous for a half-typed word ("ramil abat" also offered Bautista: two slips in four letters); the allowance for the start of a word now counts only what was typed, which removed the false match with no change in the battery.
- **A performance bug, found while testing this and fixed:** on main, at 19,360 fighters, **every English type-ahead request rebuilt the whole search index** (25 ms each, 40 requests = 1 second): the index is kept per names table, and `getNames("en")` returned a new empty object on every call, so the cache never hit. English is now one shared table (and any empty table is treated as the same one), and recent answers are cached: 1.5 ms per request, 17 times faster. Nothing in the old tests could see it.
- **What it costs and where it stops:** at 19,360 fighters an exact search is 0.5 ms, the forgiving fallback about 30 ms (15 ms when nothing is close), the index is built once per world (about 70 ms) and answers are cached; the scan grows with the number of distinct words, so expect a few times that at 100,000 fighters. Random strings far from any name find nothing (`qqqqqqqq`), but about one in five random 5-9 letter strings at this size is within a slip of some name and returns it, which is what "did you mean" does. In the ⌘K palette the guess is shown only when nothing else matched (so a typo in a page name does not list fighters beside it).
- **Checked:** types, lint, 585 tests (13 new: a hand-built league of eight fighters with near-duplicate names such as Villalba and Villalta, the battery with floors just under what was measured, the cache, the shared English table, the palette rule), smoke 148/148 on the three feeds. Seventeen deliberate breaks of the matcher (including the search palette and the shared English table) each failed a test except one, which is only a speed shortcut (the answer is the same without it).
- **Not done / limits:** only fighters are forgiving: people, gyms and events in the palette, the `/boxers?q=` name filter and the rule-based Ask planner's name matching are still exact (the AI planner's fighter lookup is forgiving); the demo league's names are synthetic and fairly regular, so real names (more diacritics, transliterations of Arabic and Cyrillic names, longer compound surnames) could do better or worse; phonetic misspellings beyond two edits ("Thompson" for "Tomsen") are not covered; and the matcher does not learn from what people click.

## 73. The backfill meets the plan's own hourly limit (round 36, 2026-10-03)

- **What happened:** the first full `vendor:backfill -- --plan` worked (44,255 fights, 11,021 events, 35,222 fighters; 496 list requests; the date-window paging read a list of 477 pages in 12 windows, its first test against the real API). ## 74. Taking the real league in stages (round 37, 2026-10-03)

- **Why:** at the plan's 500 requests an hour the 35,222 fighters take about three days (§73). The owner chose to fetch the most recently active fighters first and load only the fights between two fetched fighters, with the career-record check saying how complete it is.
- **What changed:** `--fighters N` fetches the N fighters with the most recent (or coming) fight, newest first (`lib/vendor-selection.ts`, `rankByRecency`), and loads only the fights between two of them; the rest are counted as `boutsOutsideSelection`, not as a failed fetch (that note still stops the command). A later run with a bigger N, or none, fetches only the rest from the cache. `--plan --fighters N` prices the chosen fighters and, from the fight list alone (no fighter request), prints for a few sizes the fights that would load, the share of fighters with every listed fight loaded, and how many sit in groups chosen whole. `--fighters`/`--complete-only` are refused with `--update`.
- **A finding that changes what "coherent" can mean:** a record is only certain to be right if the opponent's fights are loaded too, and so on along the chain. The first measure (every fight loaded) is what the obvious design gives, but it leaves the opponents at the edge with short records (a fighter shown as 1-0 who is 19-0-1). The strict measure, a **core** (`coherentCore`): the fighters whose records equal the vendor's totals and whose opponents' do, repeated until nothing short is left, is exactly right but is a *connected-group* property, so it is far smaller than the first measure until most of the league is fetched. `--complete-only` loads that core: no record on the site is short or invented; the league is smaller. Both numbers are in the plan, so the owner can see them before spending the hours.
- **Checked:** unit tests for the ranking (newest first, coming fights first, a long career placed by its newest fight, ties keep the list order), the preview and the core (a short record spreads along its whole chain; a conflict or a missing record puts the fighter and his opponents out; cancelled or undecided fights hold no one in or out); adapter tests on the stand-in vendor (which fighters, in what order, counted not failed, every fight loaded or counted, a bigger N costs only the rest, no limit loads everything); the real command (plan, check, refusal until allowed, flag validation, a `--complete-only` load in which **every record in the database equals the vendor's career total**). Each safeguard was broken in turn and a test caught it (oldest first, outside counted as failure, no spreading, core not applied, core not loaded, limit ignored).
- **Open:** whether the core is big enough to be worth publishing is an empirical question for the real data: the owner's first `--plan --fighters N` and `--check --fighters N` answer it. If the core is tiny at N=5,000, the choices are `--allow-partial` (short records on the site for the edge) or waiting for the full fetch; storing the vendor's career totals beside the loaded record, so a page could say "record shown is partial", is the other way and is not built.

## 85. The page checker, hardened after a security review (round 43, 2026-10-04)

- **What was reviewed:** the code that takes input from outside or calls outside services: the correction-report API and its source-quote checker, the picture and Wikidata resolvers, the vendor adapter. Authorisation, CSRF, rate limits, SQL, markup and link injection, and the handling of the API key came out sound (each checked in the code, not assumed).
- **Five real problems in the quote checker (`lib/research/fetcher.ts`, `netguard.ts`), all closed:** (1) each robots.txt rule became a regular expression, so a reporter's own robots.txt (`Disallow: /*a*a*a*a*a*a*a*a*a*a*a*c`) and a long path made one editor click stall the whole server for minutes; rules are now matched by a linear scan, checked equal to the old behaviour on 6,000 random rules, and a robots.txt is believed only up to 200 rules a group, 200 characters a rule. (2) robots.txt was fetched with redirects followed and no host check, a blind request to internal addresses (cloud metadata, localhost) from a public host's redirect; it is now fetched as the page is: same site only, at most 3 hops, each hop through the host check, anything else read as "stay away". Closing it means connecting to the checked address (pinning it in the HTTP agent), a larger change; noted in `netguard.ts`.
- **Not reviewed:** deployment (whether the reverse proxy strips `X-Forwarded-For`, which the per-client AI limit trusts, though the daily budget does not), the sports-database ingest validators, the Arabic review tooling.
- **Checked:** 9 tests (the equivalence corpus, the slow rule, the caps, the redirect cases including that the internal address is never requested and that robots.txt is asked for with manual redirects, the body cap with a stream that is cancelled, the Crawl-delay ceiling, IPv6 in every spelling, the key scrub), each fix removed in turn and a test caught it.

An empty division says "No fighter in this division has the five fights on record that a ranking needs yet." `careerView` moved to its own file (`lib/career.ts`) so the rankings can use it without a circular import; `lib/world.ts` re-exports it. `npm run smoke -- --feed partial` now checks both pages show their note.
- **Regression tests for the four accessibility fixes of §80** (the palette listbox holds only options, no bare div carries an aria-label, a cancelled bout dims only the headshots, an empty division draws no table). Each was broken in turn and a test caught it. They are source-level, like the rest of `tests/a11y.test.ts`; they do not replace an axe run.
- **Open, for the owner:** whether to change what the rankings need while the league is partial (for example three loaded fights while most careers are partial, shown as provisional), or to hold the ranking pages back until more of the league is loaded. ## 95. The numbers in a plain-English fighter search (round 51, 2026-10-04)
The rule-based search (no API key) read a sentence for a few numbers and ignored the rest, and some of what it read it read as the wrong thing. On the search as it was (`npm run search:nl`, `tests/search-nl-battery.ts`, answers checked against the league's own fighters, not against a filter list): the eight comparisons it was built for were right (8 of 8), the other 44 kinds of sentence were **0 of 44**. The worst were wrong in a way that looks like an answer: "fighters with more than 10 losses" was **the 27 undefeated fighters** (the "0 losses" inside "10 losses"); "heavyweights with a losing record" was every heavyweight (44, not 22); "fighters with 10 or more knockouts and no losses" was 60 (not 1); "fewer than 10 wins" was *at least* 10; "under 15 wins" was "at least 15 wins, aged 15 and under"; "between 20 and 30 wins" was "30 or more"; "champions from Mexico" was the list of champions of everywhere; "retired champions" the same list. Most of the rest fell through to a name search and found no one.
- **One quantity parser** (`lib/search-quantities.ts`): every number is a minimum, a maximum or both on the measure it counts, and the comparison word keeps its direction. Measures: wins, losses, knockouts, fights (bouts, "fought N times"), age, height (cm, feet and inches), reach. Words: at least / no fewer than / N or more / N+ / plus; more than / over / above; at most / no more than / up to / N or fewer; fewer than / under / below / younger / shorter; exactly; between A and B; A to B. "Lost 5 times" counts losses, "won 20 times" wins, a bare "times" fights. "None of something" in words ("no losses", "never lost", "winless", "never won by knockout", "no knockouts") is a maximum of 0.
- **Strict means strict:** "over 35" is 36 and up (as "over 33" already was), **"under 25" is 24 and down** (it had been 25), "over 6 feet" is 183 cm and up (6 ft is 182.88), "after 2015" is 2016 on and "before 2005" 2004 and back (both had included the year; "since 2015" still does). "At least" and "at most" keep their number. A bare number is still a minimum ("20 wins" is 20 or more; the chip says so), except an age ("30 years old", "aged 30").
- **Not a number of anything:** "top 5 knockout artists" (a style; it had been read as 5 knockouts), "80% knockout rate", "above 60 %", "over 200 pounds", "over 70", "top 10 knockouts". Two limits on one measure keep the stricter.
- **New filters** (`Filters`, `applyFilters`, `sanitizeFilters` for the model's own JSON, the AI prompt, the `fighters` tool's arguments, chips): `maxWins`, `maxKOs`, `maxLosses`, `minBouts`, `maxBouts`, `maxReach`, `minHeight`, `maxHeight`, `record` (winning: more wins than losses; losing: the reverse) and `champion` (`current`: holds a live belt, the same ones the champions page lists, dormant belts not counted; `former`: held one and no longer does; `ever`: has held one). "Retired champions" and "were champions" are `ever`; "former / ex / one-time champions" `former`. A fact the data does not have (a height, a reach) never satisfies a limit on it, and a belt or team filter given no world to look in matches nobody, not everybody (the team filters had matched everybody).
- **In Ask:** "champions from Mexico", "champions over 35", "former champions" are a fighter search with the belt filter, not the champions list (which has no such cut); plain "who are the champions" still is. A record list asked about any of the new cuts ("most wins among fighters with fewer than 5 losses", "among former champions", "among fighters taller than 185 cm") is the fighter search sorted the same way, or no answer, never the list for everybody (round 47's rule); "who defended the most times" is still the defences list: "champion" names that list, it does not narrow it.
- **Measured on a second batch written after the first was fitted** (group C, 43 sentences: "lost more than 5 times", "won at least 20 times", "30 and over", "ages 22 to 26", "taller than 6 ft 2", "reach between 180 and 190", "turned pro since 2020", "women with between 5 and 10 wins"): **38 of 43 (88%)** before anything was changed for it; the five misses (lost/won N times, "N cm reach or less", two "between" ranges) were fixed. All three groups now 8/8, 44/44, 43/43.
- **Checked:** types, lint, 742 tests (741 pass, 1 skipped as before; 10 new in `tests/search-nl.test.ts`: the battery against the league, a guard that no battery truth is empty or everyone, every reading pinned, the rounding of feet, repeated limits, styles and rates, champions against the lines of live belts, the planner's routes, the model's filters, the chips in both languages, unknown heights; three older expectations changed because over/under are now strict), 12 Arabic strings, build, smoke 177/177. **Old-versus-new plans over 2,164 questions** (the whole Ask battery, the new sentences, and ten subjects × thirty cuts × five openings): 1,273 plans changed, none that had an answer lost it, and none carries a number in the question that is missing from the arguments; the changes are the qualifiers now applied. **39 deliberate mutations** (direction and rounding of each comparison, intersection of limits, suffix words, styles and ranks, feet, champion sets, unknown facts, routing): 17 survived the first tests, each got a test, and all are killed now but one that cannot differ (the order the comparison words are tried in: only one is ever set).
- **Found on the way:** "best|top" matched inside "stopped", so "boxers who were never stopped" was a request for the best (everyone, by rating); both are whole words now.
- **Not done / limits:** Arabic numbers in a search are still read only for wins, knockouts, reach and "undefeated" (the new cuts are English; an Arabic sentence with them finds no one rather than the wrong people); "never been knocked out" (a chin, not a count of knockouts won) is not a filter yet, and is deliberately not read as "no knockouts"; weight in pounds or kilograms, "in the last year" and "N rounds" are not read; a bare number with no word after it is not guessed.
